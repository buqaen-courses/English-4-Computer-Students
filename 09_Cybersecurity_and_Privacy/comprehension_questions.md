# Security Reflections: Protecting Digital Assets

**Your Security Journey:**

1. **Security Awakening:** What's the first cybersecurity incident or concept that made you realize digital security matters? How did it change your online behavior or development practices?

2. **Privacy Evolution:** How has your understanding of online privacy changed over the years? What specific experiences (data breaches, tracking revelations) influenced your current privacy practices?

3. **Attack Awareness:** Share a cybersecurity attack or vulnerability you've read about that surprised you. What made it surprising, and what lessons did you take from it?

**Core Security Concepts:**

4. **CIA Triad Application:** Choose a system or service you use daily (banking app, social media, email). Analyze how it implements the CIA triad. Where does it excel, and where could it improve?

5. **Attack Vector Analysis:** Pick a common attack type from the article (phishing, ransomware, SQL injection). Explain how it works in simple terms and describe prevention strategies for developers and users.

6. **Encryption Scenarios:** Compare symmetric and asymmetric encryption using a real-world example. When would you choose one over the other for securing data?

**Privacy and Ethics:**

7. **Privacy Impact Assessment:** Imagine you're building a fitness tracking app. What personal data would you collect, how would you protect it, and what privacy concerns should you address?

8. **Bias in Security:** How might security systems inadvertently discriminate against certain user groups? Give examples and suggest ways to ensure fairness.

9. **Zero Trust Evaluation:** How would implementing zero trust principles change your approach to securing a web application? What specific changes would you make?

**Practical Security:**

10. **Vulnerability Assessment:** Read about a recent security vulnerability (from CVE database or news). Summarize it in your own words and explain its potential impact.

11. **Incident Response Planning:** You're responsible for a small web application. Outline a basic incident response plan for a potential security breach.

12. **Authentication Design:** Compare different authentication methods (password-only, MFA, biometrics) for a banking application. What trade-offs would you consider?

**Research and Communication:**

13. **Security Advisory Drafting:** Write a brief security advisory for a hypothetical vulnerability in a popular library. Include the problem, impact, and recommended actions.

14. **Privacy Policy Analysis:** Read the privacy policy of a service you use. How well does it explain data collection, usage, and user rights? What improvements would you suggest?

**Modern Threats and Defenses:**

15. **Emerging Threat Response:** The article mentions AI-powered attacks and supply chain vulnerabilities. How would you prepare a development team to defend against these threats?

16. **IoT Security Challenges:** What unique security challenges do Internet of Things (IoT) devices present compared to traditional computers? How would you secure a smart home system?

**Professional Development:**

17. **Security Career Paths:** Which cybersecurity role from the article interests you most, and why? What skills would you need to develop to pursue that path?

18. **Security Culture Building:** How would you promote security awareness within a development team? What specific practices or habits would you encourage?

**Ethical Considerations:**

19. **Responsible Disclosure Dilemma:** You discover a security vulnerability in a widely-used open-source library. How would you handle the responsible disclosure process?

20. **Privacy vs. Security Trade-offs:** In what situations might privacy and security goals conflict? How would you balance these competing interests?

**Instructor Notes:**
- Encourage students to share personal security experiences without revealing sensitive details
- Connect security concepts to students' current projects and daily digital habits
- Discuss the societal implications of cybersecurity decisions
- Help students understand that security is about balancing protection with usability

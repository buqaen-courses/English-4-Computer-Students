# Research Reading Reflections

**Connecting with the Journey:**

1. **The Opening Panic:** The article begins with a scenario of feeling overwhelmed by a research paper. Share a time when you felt similarly lost while reading academic material. What was the paper about, and how did you eventually overcome the confusion?

2. **Your Reading Evolution:** How has your approach to reading research papers changed since you started your studies? What strategies from the article do you already use, and which ones are you excited to try?

3. **The Detective Story Analogy:** The article compares research papers to detective novels. Can you think of another metaphor that might help explain research papers to beginners? How would this metaphor change how someone approaches reading?

**Critical Engagement:**

4. **Question Everything:** The article discusses being a "bullshit detector." What red flags do you look for when reading research papers? Share an example of a paper where you spotted something suspicious.

5. **Purpose-Driven Reading:** How do you currently decide which papers to read deeply versus which ones to skim? How might adopting the three-pass approach change your research habits?

**Practical Application:**

6. **Your Research Arsenal:** What tools do you currently use for managing research papers and notes? Which ones from the article's recommendations are you interested in trying?

7. **The Conversation Analogy:** Research papers are part of an ongoing conversation. Think about a topic you're interested in - how would tracing citations help you understand the current state of research in that area?

**Personal Growth:**

8. **From Consumer to Contributor:** The article talks about shifting from being a passive reader to an active contributor. What small step could you take this week to move closer to this goal?

**Group Discussion Starters:**

9. **Paper Swap Challenge:** In groups, have each person bring a research paper they found challenging. Take turns explaining the paper's main contribution using the three-pass approach.

10. **Red Flag Hunt:** As a group, examine a recent paper and discuss potential methodological concerns or overreaching claims.

**Instructor Notes:**
- Encourage sharing personal struggles and victories
- Connect reading strategies to students' current research projects
- Discuss how critical reading skills transfer to other academic work
- Emphasize that becoming a skilled paper reader takes time and practice

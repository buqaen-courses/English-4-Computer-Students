# Research Paper Mastery Quiz

**Reading Strategy Scenarios:**

1. **The Time Crunch:** You have 2 hours to prepare for a meeting where you'll discuss recent advances in your field. You find 15 potentially relevant papers. How do you prioritize which ones to read and what reading strategy do you use for each?

2. **The Confusing Math Paper:** You're reading a paper with complex mathematical proofs, but the introduction suggests it's highly relevant to your work. The equations are going over your head. What's your next step?

3. **The Controversial Results:** You encounter a paper claiming a breakthrough result that contradicts several well-established studies. How do you evaluate whether this is a genuine discovery or methodological flaw?

**Critical Analysis Challenges:**

4. **The Perfect Results:** A paper reports 99.9% accuracy on a well-studied dataset. What questions should you ask about their methodology and results?

5. **The Small Sample:** A psychology paper draws broad conclusions from a study of only 23 participants. What are the potential issues with this approach?

6. **The Missing Baseline:** You're reviewing a paper that proposes a new algorithm but doesn't compare it against existing state-of-the-art methods. How does this affect your assessment of the work?

**Practical Application:**

7. **The Literature Review:** You're writing a literature review and have collected 50 papers on your topic. How do you organize this material and identify the key themes and gaps?

8. **The Conference Talk:** You need to give a 15-minute presentation summarizing recent work in your field. How do you select and synthesize information from multiple research papers?

**Tool Integration:**

9. **The Digital Workflow:** Describe how you would use at least three different tools (reference manager, note-taking app, PDF reader) to manage and analyze a collection of research papers for a systematic review.

**Research Ethics and Rigor:**

10. **The Reproducibility Crisis:** A highly-cited paper in your field has results that other researchers can't replicate. How do you approach reading and citing this work in your own research?

**Bonus - Real Research Scenarios:**

11. **The Grant Proposal:** You're writing a grant proposal and need to demonstrate that your research question is novel and important. How do you use research papers to build this case?

12. **The Peer Review:** You're asked to review a submitted paper for a conference. What are the key elements you look for in evaluating its contribution, methodology, and presentation?
